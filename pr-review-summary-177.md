# PR Review: Matcher: infer amendment lineage behind `--infer-lineage` (partial #170); split the status vocabulary (#183)

## Verdict: REQUEST CHANGES

## Overview

This PR does two separable things. The first adds three rules that guess which debt instrument replaced which, filling in amendment pointers that the extraction stage cannot express because it only ever looks at one filing at a time. The second replaces the eight flat `status` values with five, moving the reason an instrument closed into a new `status_subtype` column. The second half is good work and I would merge it close to as-is. The first half has a bug that publishes live borrowing as dead, and I do not think it should ship in its current form.

Start with what holds up, because it is a lot. **Every headline number in the PR description reproduces exactly.** I re-ran the rules and re-derived the statuses against the run output already on disk: 15 links split 13/1/1 across the three rules, 2 children correctly refused as ambiguous, lineage heads 537 down to 528, `superseded_by` 5 up to 10, EQT 68 to 66, MPLX 29 to 28, and all five status counts. That almost never happens, and it is the only reason this review could be as specific as it is. The suite passes (449 tests, 1 expected failure), the linter and formatter are clean at the repo's pinned version, and all five CI checks are green. The "Honest limits" section is genuinely honest: it declines to claim #170 is closed, names the real gap against the hand-reviewed instrument counts, and says out loud that reading filing text inside the matcher is the wrong long-term home. A mutation test of the status half killed 17 of 24 targeted edits, so the PR's claim of "five mutations each fail a test" is not just true, it is understated.

Four things block merge, and all four are in the lineage half. **The worst is that the `dated_reference` rule invents links inside a single filing.** Its trigger list includes "amended and restated", which matches the instrument's *own* name, so the date it captures is usually the instrument's own signing date rather than a predecessor's. I built the minimal case — two tranches of one brand-new credit agreement, with no predecessor in existence anywhere — and the rule linked them as parent and child. On the real data, 13 of the 15 links share a filing with their supposed parent, and in one case four unrelated promissory notes were all adopted by a single parent from one filing's text. **The consequence is the thing this feature exists to prevent:** PENN's Second Amended and Restated Credit Agreement — $962.5M, maturing 2033-05-31, which the filing describes as amended and continuing — is published as `closed - superseded` by two of its own tranches, which that same filing says are unchanged. The two rows that stay lineage heads have no start date, no maturity and no principal, so a head-filtered browse index shows two empty stubs and hides the real facility. A wrongly-inferred link also *lowers* the head count, which is the exact number used to demonstrate success — so the improvement from 537 to 528 and this bug are not separable without auditing all 13 links by hand.

The other two blockers are narrower but concrete. **Three of the fifteen link directions are decided by a hash.** When the rules produce both directions for a pair, the cycle breaker keeps whichever one it reaches first in sorted order; I confirmed this by renaming instruments so they sort differently and watching the surviving direction flip all three times. For one issuer the two rules actively disagreed about which instrument came first, and the tie went to the identifier rather than being refused. That contradicts the PR's own stated principle that an ambiguous guess is worse than doing nothing. **And the `amendment_inferred_by` column is erased by the next ordinary `cdt match`, while the pointer it explains survives.** That column exists for exactly one reason — so a guessed pointer is never mistaken for an extracted one — and it lasts until the next routine pipeline run, after which the 15 guesses are indistinguishable from extractor output and can only be removed with `--force`. Three reviewers found this independently.

Two more findings are worth your attention even though they do not block. The status change is **not** behind the flag: 125 of 542 rows (23%) change their `status` string whether or not you pass `--infer-lineage`, and the values `matured`, `repaid`, `terminated`, `exchanged` and `defaulted` disappear from that column entirely. `MATCHER_SCHEMA_VERSION` is still 4, though #178 — the commit directly below this one — bumped it 3 to 4 for a smaller change, and the repo's own test says the version is how a downstream reader learns a rebuild is needed. The dashboard is a separate repository that reads these values, so without a bump the break is silent. Separately, and more strategically: since 13 of 15 links join instruments named in the *same* filing, 87% of the measured benefit sits inside the scope the extraction stage already covers. That undercuts the stated reason for letting the matcher read filing text at all, and it suggests the PR's own note that #167 is the right home is the more accurate diagnosis than its problem statement.

The test coverage picture is sharply uneven, and that unevenness is itself the finding. Of 50 mutations I applied, 25 were caught. But the status legs caught 71% while the lineage plumbing caught 14%. `apply_lineage_inference_pass` — the only function the command-line actually calls, which rewrites every published partition on disk — has no test at all, and four separate edits turn it into a silent no-op with a green suite. The `--infer-lineage` flag can be fully **inverted** and nothing fails. To be fair on severity: the HIPPO scale puts missing tests at Important, not High, so these are not what drives the verdict. The verdict is driven by the four bugs above. But they cluster on the same untested function, which is why they were all still there to find.

How this review was done, since it affects how much to trust each claim: five focused reviewers each took one concern (correctness, test coverage, repo coherence, performance and data safety, and the seam between the two commits), and I re-checked every finding against the full file before it reached this summary — dropping or downgrading what did not survive, and recording nine hypotheses that turned out to be wrong. I personally reproduced all four High findings. Every number quoted here came from a command that was actually run, against the artifacts already on disk, with no new model calls. Two process notes: I archived the previous review of #178 to `pr-review-findings-178.md` and `pr-review-summary-178.md` before writing these files, and mutation testing ran in a throwaway git worktree — afterwards I confirmed no tracked file in this checkout was modified and the suite still passes.

## Inline notes

### `src/cdt/matcher/lineage_inference.py`

- **L65, L236-252** `HIGH` `[correctness]` **`dated_reference` links two instruments from the same filing as parent and child** — The trigger list includes `amend\w+\s+and\s+restat\w+`, which matches the child's *own* name, so the captured "dated as of" date is usually its own signing date. Every open instrument in that filing then inherits it and gets offered the same parent. Minimal repro: one filing describing a Term Loan A and a Revolver from one new agreement, no predecessor anywhere, yields `{'i-tl': ('i-rv', 'dated_reference')}`. On real data 13 of 15 links share a filing with their parent, and NMP had four unrelated notes adopted by one parent. Nothing caps children-per-parent, so fan-in is unbounded. Suggested fix: subtract the child's own identity dates before offering (`referenced -= identity.get(child_id, set())`), or require the referenced date to be strictly earlier than all of the child's own dates. The existing `dated_reference` test stays green either way.
- **L254-286** `HIGH` `[correctness]` **Three of the fifteen link directions are decided by instrument-ID sort order** — Where the rules offer both directions for a pair, each child sees exactly one candidate, so the ambiguity guard never fires; the cycle breaker then deletes whichever link it reaches first in `sorted(resolved)`. I renamed one member of each pair to sort last and the surviving direction flipped in all three cases. For TIPTREE the two rules disagreed outright — `ordinal_chain` said A amends B, `dated_reference` said the reverse — and the contradiction was settled by the hash. Direction decides which row is published as closed. Suggested fix: when a mutual pair is found, drop **both** links. A cycle is evidence the rules cannot tell which instrument came first, which is precisely when the PR's own rule says to do nothing.
- **L220-228** `IMPORTANT` `[correctness]` **A rank tie picks an arbitrary predecessor, and the ambiguity guard cannot see it** — The guard counts candidates *per child*, but `ordinal_chain` only ever offers `members[index - 1]` after sorting. Two equal-rank predecessors are silently reduced to one by sort order instead of being refused. Verified: two bare "Amended and Restated Credit Agreement" rows plus a "Second Amended and Restated" produces one link with `ambiguous=0`; on a date tie the winner is whichever identifier sorts larger. This contradicts the documented promise to refuse a child with more than one candidate parent, and the PR notes that over-split clusters — which is what creates same-rank duplicates — exist in this corpus. Suggested fix: offer every member sharing the preceding rank, so the existing guard refuses the link.
- **L95-100** `IMPORTANT` `[correctness]` **`_iso` is case-sensitive while the regex above it is not, so some links vanish silently** — `DATED_REFERENCE` is compiled with `IGNORECASE`, so its month group matches any capitalization; `_iso` then re-matches case-sensitively and returns nothing. `July 7, 2022` resolves; `july 7, 2022`, `JULY 7, 2022` and `Jul 7, 2022` all return `None`. All-caps preambles are routine in credit agreement exhibits, and there is no log line when a link is dropped this way. Best fix is to reuse `normalized_date_from_text` from `extractor/core.py:3675`, which handles this already — see the duplication note below.
- **L73-100** `IMPORTANT` `[repo-coherence]` **`_iso` is a third copy of month-name date parsing, and the weakest of the three** — `normalize_date` (`matcher/core.py:2116`) and `normalized_date_from_text` (`extractor/core.py:3675`, with `MONTH_MAP` at `:407`) already do this, and the extractor's version tolerates an optional comma, odd spacing, and validates the result. Reusing it would also **fix** the case-sensitivity bug above. One caveat that makes this real work rather than a rename: `core.py:33` imports this module, so it cannot import back without a cycle — the cheap route is to pass normalized values in from `apply_lineage_inference_pass`.
- **L123-133, L147-154** `IMPORTANT` `[repo-coherence]` **Two code paths are dead because `PreparedMention` has no `dates_json` field, and `getattr` hides it** — Confirmed by reflecting over the dataclass: `amounts_json` is present, `dates_json` is not. Both readers use `getattr(mention, column, None)`, so a missing field looks exactly like an empty one. So `prior_fact` sees prior amounts only — while still comparing them against each candidate's `maturity_date`, which can only match by accident — and `_identity_dates` falls back to `start_date` alone, meaning the `agreement`/`closing` branch that implements the #167 idea never runs. The PR discloses the gap honestly; the finding is that the code is written as if the field existed and fails quietly when it does not. Suggested fix: add the field (the PR says this "would likely lift both rules"), or drop the dead branches and correct the module docstring. If you keep them for later, use direct attribute access so the absence raises instead of silently returning nothing.
- **L132** `IMPORTANT` `[tests]` **No test checks that the `prior` mark is what makes a `prior_fact` link** — Removing `payload.get("prior") and`, so every amount counts, survives the whole suite. The existing test's mention carries a marked `2000000000` and an unmarked `3000000000`, and with the guard gone the unmarked value only offers the child to itself, which a different check rejects. The rule's entire premise is unverified. Suggested fix: add a case where an **unmarked** amount equals an earlier instrument's principal and assert no link is produced.
- **L227** `IMPORTANT` `[tests]` **Loosening the ordinal comparison invents lineage, and no test catches it** — Changing `rank > prev_rank` to `>=` survives, as does deleting the `MIN_CHAIN_MEMBERS` guard, because the only ordinal test uses strictly increasing ranks (0, 2, 3). The loosened version is not harmless: two rows both named "Credit Agreement" go from no link to `{'b': ('a', 'ordinal_chain')}`. Given that a wrong pointer rewrites published history, the strict comparison is exactly the line that needs a negative test.
- **L150-154** `IMPORTANT` `[tests]` **The `agreement`/`closing` date-kind filter is untested** — Accepting any date kind survives, because the single `dated_reference` test resolves against `mention.start_date` and never against a `dates_json` payload. Widening this would let a predecessor be matched on its *maturity* date, which is one way a dated reference picks the wrong parent. Note this filter is currently unreachable anyway, per the dead-code note above.
- **L58, L103-113** `PREF` `[repo-coherence]` **Name normalization is re-implemented here and diverges from the matcher's own** — `NAME_NOISE` and the `lower().split()` handling duplicate `normalize_name_fingerprint` (`matcher/core.py:2148`) and `NAME_STOPWORDS` (`:2286`); the two stopword sets share only `{the, certain}`. Because this works off the raw `name` column rather than a fingerprint, it keeps punctuation the matcher deliberately strips, so "Credit Agreement, dated…" and "Credit Agreement dated…" get different stems while the matcher treats them as the same name. Same import-direction caveat as above.
- **L188-192** `OPINION` `[correctness]` **The date-ordering guard treats a missing date asymmetrically** — A parent with no `first_seen_filing_date` is treated as oldest and accepted; a child with no date is treated as oldest and rejects every dated parent. Both follow one convention (empty string sorts first), so this is coherent rather than wrong. The permissive direction — an undated cluster becoming a parent on no ordering evidence — is a small soundness gap given the "conservative" framing. No change requested; flagging in case you want the guard to be strict both ways.
- **L205-209** `OPINION` `[performance]` **The `terms` set is rebuilt for every `(child, other)` pair** — 7,396 set constructions for the largest single issuer on disk (86 instruments). Precomputing per row makes it linear. Irrelevant at today's scale; noting it since the surrounding loop is already the quadratic part.

### `src/cdt/matcher/core.py`

- **L775-781** `HIGH` `[correctness]` **An inferred link can leave a whole lineage family with no live row** — The superseded leg sits above every date leg and never asks whether the *child* is a plausible successor. On real data, PENN's `Second Amended and Restated Credit Agreement` ($962.5M, matures **2033**-05-31, reference date 2026-09-08) is now `closed - superseded` by two of its own tranches, so the only rows left as lineage heads are stubs with no dates and no principal. Two rows became `closed - superseded` while carrying a future maturity (PENN and Rent the Runway). This is the exact symptom #170 was opened about, reproduced by #170's own fix. **Corrected 2026-09-15:** this note originally cited MPLX; those two MPLX rows turned out to be the same facility in two unmerged clusters, so closing it is coincidentally right and only the published *relation* is false — see the addendum in `pr-review-findings.md`. Credit where due: precedence is right where it matters most — an extracted terminal event still outranks an inferred `superseded`, so a guess cannot overwrite a recorded fact. Suggested fix: refuse a link whose child is already terminal at an earlier date, or require a plausible-successor check before this leg fires.
- **L1598-1655** `HIGH` `[data-safety]` **The next ordinary `cdt match` erases `amendment_inferred_by` but keeps the pointer** — `derive_parent_links` deliberately carries `amendment_of_debt_instrument_id` forward (`L1471-1476`), but `build_debt_instrument_rows` never emits `amendment_inferred_by`, so the reindex nulls it. Measured end to end: pointers 15 non-null before and after, provenance 15 before and **0** after. Found independently by three reviewers. The column exists so that "an inferred pointer is never mistaken for an extracted one" — and that holds until the next routine run, after which `derive_parent_links` treats all 15 guesses as authoritative existing evidence that only `--force` can clear. Suggested fix: carry it forward alongside the pointer, cleared when the pointer changes. One line.
- **L2417** `IMPORTANT` `[data-safety]` **`read_item_texts` silently reads nothing from an `s3://` artifact root** — It builds its path with `Path(str(artifact_root)) / "classifications"` and then globs. `--artifact-root` explicitly accepts an S3 URI (`cli.py:79`), and `pathlib` collapses the double slash, so `Path("s3://bucket/root")` becomes the local-looking `s3:/bucket/root` and the glob matches nothing without raising. Verified: `read_item_texts('s3://my-bucket/cdt-artifacts')` returns `{}`. Since rule 3 is guarded by `if item_texts:`, that silently disables the rule producing **13 of 15 links**, with no warning — the per-file `LOGGER.warning` never fires because the loop never runs. Every sibling read in the same function goes through `read_dataset`, which handles S3 fine. Suggested fix: `read_dataset(classifications_root(artifact_root, data_dir=data_dir), columns=["item_id", "text"])`, reusing the existing helper and constant from `classifier/core.py:44,77`. That also drops the hardcoded partition layout. I considered High and settled on Important because the feature is off by default and the failure loses links rather than publishing wrong ones — and, ironically, on S3 it also avoids the over-linking bug above.
- **L2452-2461, L2476-2478** `IMPORTANT` `[data-safety]` **A partly readable edges dataset silently blanks observation columns on rows in unrelated partitions** — The guard is all-or-nothing (`if instruments.empty or edges.empty or mentions.empty`), so a *partial* edges dataset passes it, and the rollup then overwrites `mention_count`, `document_count`, `first_seen_filing_date`, `last_seen_filing_date`, `status` and `status_subtype` from edges alone with no cross-check. Measured by deleting one edges partition holding 78 of 542 instruments' members: all 78 lost their counts (to 0) and both dates (to `None`), `status` changed on 25 rows, exit code 0, no warning. In the normal match path this damage is bounded to the shard being rebuilt; here the pass rewrites every partition, so one bad file corrupts rows it has no business touching. Suggested fix: before writing, assert every instrument id appears in `member_groups` (or that total `mention_count` did not fall) and abort instead of publishing.
- **L46** `IMPORTANT` `[migrations]` **Two columns added and the `status` vocabulary replaced, with `MATCHER_SCHEMA_VERSION` still 4** — 125 of 542 rows (23%) change their `status` string unconditionally, and `matured`, `superseded`, `repaid`, `terminated`, `exchanged` and `defaulted` all disappear as `status` values. The precedent directly below this PR is unambiguous: #178 (`67a4db4`) bumped 3 to 4 with the message "Bumps MATCHER_SCHEMA_VERSION to 4 for the debt-instruments column change", and `f3b1ed5` bumped 2 to 3 before that. This change is strictly larger. The repo's own test says "The version is how a downstream reader learns a rebuild is required" (`tests/test_file_native_stages.py:5866`), and the constant is stamped into the match manifest (`L392`) and the `latest.json` snapshot pointer (`pipeline.py:571`) the dashboard resolves. Suggested fix: `MATCHER_SCHEMA_VERSION = 5`.
- **L648, L2476** `IMPORTANT` `[correctness]` **Two writers disagree about "today", so `--infer-lineage` moves statuses on rows with no inferred link** — `apply_lifecycle_rollup` takes its reference date from whatever mention index it is handed. `match_tables` hands it one `cik_shard`; the pass hands it the whole corpus. Per-shard maxima span 2026-05-05 to 2026-09-08 across 63 shards. Re-deriving the same 542 rows both ways differs on 3, and turning the flag on with **zero** inferred links still moved `active` to `expected_closed`. Two consequences: the PR's #183 table is the corpus-wide number, not what the pipeline publishes — including the *Orrstown* spot-check, which the pipeline actually publishes as `active` — and the pass's result is partly reverted by the next ordinary run. Also makes `docs/schema.md:271`'s reproducibility promise false in the shard path. Suggested fix: compute the reference date once per run and pass it in explicitly, which makes both paths agree and the doc true.
- **L823-824** `IMPORTANT` `[correctness]` **The `expected_active` leg skips the end-date check that the `active` leg applies** — Leg 4 tests `past_every_end` before returning `active`; leg 5 does not. So a row whose planned start *and* whose maturity are both in the past publishes as "probably alive". Verified on two rows differing only in whether the start was confirmed: expected start 2019-06-01 with maturity 2023-01-01 at reference 2026-01-01 gives `expected_active`, while the same row with an explicit start gives `expected_closed`. Reachable for any never-confirmed note with a name-derived maturity now past ("6.0% Senior Notes due 2023"), and the PR reports 19 rows moving into `expected_active`. Suggested fix: hoist the `past_every_end` return above line 823 so it covers both legs. Existing tests carry no end dates and stay green.
- **L2430, L2480-2490** `IMPORTANT` `[data-safety]` **The pass rewrites all 63 partitions without renewing the writer lease** — `match_pending_mentions` calls `renew()` before each shard rewrite (`L317-318`) and its docstring says why: "a full match pass can outlast the pipeline-writer lease TTL, and it must raise rather than let this run keep rewriting shards a lease thief now owns (#89)." The pass takes no `renew` and has no ownership check, yet rewrites *every* partition — more than the normal path — and runs after the match phase, when the lease is closest to expiring. Its signature also breaks the stage convention (positional `str`, no `data_dir`, no `renew`) shared by all four other stage entry points. The rewrite is also not atomic, so a mid-run failure leaves the dataset internally inconsistent with nothing recording it. Suggested fix: `apply_lineage_inference_pass(artifact_root, *, data_dir=None, renew=None)` and call `renew()` in the shard loop.
- **L419-425, L271-287** `IMPORTANT` `[repo-coherence]` **`match_tables` and `match_pending_mentions` accept `infer_lineage`/`item_texts` and read neither** — Both appear only in the signature and docstring; `infer_amendment_parents` is never called in `match_tables`. `match_pending_mentions` loads all item text and never forwards it — measured at 11.06 s and 33.3 MB peak across 1,554 parquet reads for zero effect, paid *before* the mentions read so it happens even when the early return at `L294` fires. The docstring actively promises behavior that does not exist, which recreates the exact trap the PR body describes hitting: "My first wiring returned zero links for exactly this reason, with no error." Both functions are public API. Suggested fix: delete both parameters and the docstring paragraph; the post-pass is the only supported entry point.
- **L117** `IMPORTANT` `[data-safety]` **`amendment_inferred_by` flips parquet type between `double` and `string` from run to run** — Because nothing sets the key in the normal path, the reindex materializes an all-NaN `float64` column (parquet `double`); the pass writes real strings. Measured: `{'double': 63}` after a plain match, `{'null': 56, 'string': 7}` after the pass, and in the *published* snapshot the same column is `double` one generation and `string` the next. Combined with the provenance-wipe bug above, it flips on every run. Nothing in-repo fails (the repo's reader is a pandas concat), but a typed external table breaks. Fixing the provenance carry-forward stabilizes this to `string` for free.
- **L2430-2500** `IMPORTANT` `[tests]` **The only function the CLI calls has no test, and four edits make it a silent no-op** — `grep -rn "apply_lineage_inference_pass" tests/` returns nothing. Surviving mutations: gutting the pointer write, deleting the rollup re-derive, never writing partitions at all, and passing `item_texts=None` to disable `dated_reference`. The last two matter most — the rollup re-derive is what the PR says keeps the lifecycle columns consistent, and `dated_reference` is 13 of 15 links. Every defect in the data-safety notes above lives in this function. A 35-line test on the existing `test_match_pending_mentions_writes_match_datasets` pattern was written, run green, and confirmed to kill three of the four. Graded Important per the HIPPO scale's placement of missing tests, with the doubt noted: that is unusually cheap coverage for the risk.
- **L2430-2504, L377-393** `IMPORTANT` `[repo-coherence]` **Nothing records that lineage was inferred** — The match manifest records every other knob (batch size, all three thresholds, schema version, partitions written) but nothing anywhere records `infer_lineage`, and the pass writes no manifest. Worse than an omission: the pass runs *after* `match_pending_mentions` writes that manifest, so the manifest describes a dataset the pass then silently changes. Stage manifests are called out as a design property in `docs/architecture.md:38`. Per-row `amendment_inferred_by` is not a substitute — it says nothing about rows where no rule fired, and it does not survive the next match anyway. Suggested fix: add `infer_lineage` to the manifest dict at `L383-393`, and/or rewrite the manifest at the end of the pass with the counts it already returns.
- **L832-833** `IMPORTANT` `[tests]` **The `announced`-on-a-future-start leg has no test** — Making it return `active` survives the suite. This is a real branch, not a boundary nuance: it decides whether an instrument that does not exist yet is published as live or as announced. Every other new leg has a dedicated test. One `derive_instrument_status` call with a start date after the reference date and `event_result=None` closes it.
- **L2407-2427** `IMPORTANT` `[tests]` **`read_item_texts` is untested, and three mutations survive** — No test references it. Returning `{}`, renaming the directory to `classificationz`, and narrowing the glob all survive. It hardcodes both the literal `"classifications"` and the `date=*/shard=*/*.parquet` layout instead of using `CLASSIFICATION_DATASET_NAME` and `dataset_root(...)`, so a partitioning change turns 87% of the feature into a no-op behind one warning. - **L745, L813, L816, L849, L879-884, L2037** `PREF` `[tests]` **Boundary and tie-break conditions are untested** — Six surviving mutations, all off-by-one or tie-break. Every date comparison is tested strictly inside its range, never at equality — and since the reference date *is* the newest filing date in the run, equality is routine rather than a corner case: an instrument whose start date is the newest filing date flips between `active` and `announced`. Likewise `planned_retirement_date`'s `>` at equality (a same-day filing announcing and effecting a redemption) flips `closed` to `expected_closed`; `min()` on duplicate expected closings is called "the conservative read" with no test supplying two; and `expected_start_for_instrument` can be reversed to oldest-first with nothing failing. Every other partition read goes through it unguarded. This one also drops the cause entirely, so the rule quietly degrades to "fewer links". The S3 fix above removes the need for it.

This PR's own `status_subtype` follows that convention, which makes the inconsistency internal to the diff. ## What I verified and found fine

Recorded in full in `pr-review-findings.md`; the highlights, because absence of a finding should mean "checked", not "did not look":

- **Nine hypotheses I tested and refuted.** The most notable: I suspected `prior_fact`'s string comparison was type-broken (`"2000000000.0"` vs `"2000000000"`). It is not — `normalize_numeric_string` canonicalizes through `Decimal.normalize()`, so "$2.0 billion", "$2,000,000,000" and "2000000000" all become the same string, and a full end-to-end run produced a real link. The 1-of-15 yield is conservatism, not a bug. Also refuted: the `prior`-marker asymmetry in `expected_dates_from_dates_json` (the extractor rejects `prior` on every event date kind, so it cannot leak), `planned_retirement_date`'s dependence on `mention.date` (never null in real data), and a suspected stale-file duplication in the partition rewrite (every partition on disk holds exactly one file).
- **The `is_lineage_head` leg is sound.** I suspected the new `closed - superseded` leg could misfire on split children. It cannot: the head flag is derived solely from amendment children (`core.py:660` over `L614-618`), and `lineage_family_id` is the value that spans split and retirement edges.
- **The cascade has no dead legs and cannot leave its vocabulary.** An exhaustive sweep of 11,664 input combinations returned only the five published values, with every leg reached.
- **The pass is idempotent and recovers from a partial failure.** A second run reports 0 links and byte-identical output; after an interrupted run it converges to exactly the clean result.
- **No regular-expression denial-of-service risk.** Doubling the input gives flat milliseconds-per-megabyte (1779, 2166, 2347, 2478, 2104), so behavior is linear. The largest real filing on disk takes under 0.18 s even at pathological density.
- **Complexity is fine at the real scale.** The largest single issuer on disk has 86 instruments (90th percentile is 5); the per-issuer quadratic term costs about 0.02 s there and only becomes visible above roughly 500, six times the largest real filer.
- **A partition-relocation duplication I found is pre-existing, not this PR's.** A legacy-layout root gives 550 rows and 8 duplicates — but a plain `cdt match` on the identical root produces exactly the same, so it is a `shard_for_cik` hazard rather than something introduced here.
